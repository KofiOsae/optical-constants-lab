Theory & interpretation
Rayleigh vs Mie
Size parameter x=2π n_host r / λ₀. Rayleigh is a small-particle approximation requiring small x and small internal phase variation; Mie solves the homogeneous sphere boundary-value problem across size regimes. Neither solves arbitrary particle shapes.
Efficiencies
Cᵢ = Qᵢ πr² and Qext = Qsca + Qabs for passive spheres. Ensemble efficiencies use number-averaged geometric area.
Directional transport
g = ⟨cos θ⟩ weighted by differential scattering cross-section; μs = N ⟨Csca⟩; μs′ = μs(1−g). For polydisperse ensembles, g is scattering-weighted, not number-weighted.
Applicability
The model assumes spherical particles, a lossless homogeneous host, and independent scattering. At high concentrations, interparticle interactions and multiple scattering can invalidate macroscopic coefficients. Effective-medium predictions and Mie attenuation must not be combined without checking for double counting.
