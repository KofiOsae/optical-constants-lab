Guided spectral-analysis tutorial
First verify wavelength and R/T units. For a passive sample, total-power absorptance is A = 1 − R − T; negative values beyond uncertainty indicate calibration or modeling problems. Interference fringe spacing constrains optical thickness n·d, but does not uniquely determine n and d separately.
Phase I: select a weak-absorption interval, fit a physically plausible Fresnel model, inspect reconstructed R/T, thickness and parameters. Phase II: use the Phase I seed to fit broadband n and k and inspect held-out error. Swanepoel: requires resolved transmission extrema and a transparent substrate with n > 1; it may fail on strongly absorbing or fringe-free spectra. Tauc: a model-dependent absorption-edge analysis, not a complete n,k inversion.
Optical constants satisfy ε′=n²−k², ε″=2nk and α=4πk/λ (with λ in the same length unit as α⁻¹). Small R/T residuals do not establish unique n,k. Compare independent thicknesses, polarizations and angles where possible.
